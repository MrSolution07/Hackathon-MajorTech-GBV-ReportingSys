# SafeStep

Read the two speeches. Do not read the canvas or the sources on stage.

## Problem

About 1 minute 30 seconds.

Three women a day. That is how the South African Medical Research Council counted women killed by an intimate partner in 2020 and 2021. Not a stranger in an alley. Someone who already knew where she slept.

The harm follows her phone. In a study of women online, 38 percent had lived through online violence, and 85 percent had watched it happen to another woman. United Nations Women puts the spread across studies at 16 to 58 percent. In five countries in sub-Saharan Africa, 28 percent of women reported online violence. In Gqeberha, one study of schoolgirls found 43 percent of secondary pupils and 11 percent of primary pupils had faced technology-facilitated abuse in a year. One city. Not the nation. Still a warning.

South Africa already wrote the law. The Cybercrimes Act. The Protection from Harassment Act. The file still falls apart. From April to December 2024, police recorded 2,884 women murdered, 31,303 rapes, and 75,944 domestic-violence crimes against women. From January 2024 to March 2025, 169,462 domestic-violence cases were opened. 43,292 were withdrawn.

She is holding screenshots in five apps, afraid of the wrong call, with nothing that still exists on Monday. The law did not gather those screenshots into one place. That is the gap. Not another slogan. The file.

## Solution

About 1 minute.

SafeStep is that file. She pastes the message. The app names the pattern it can see: a threat, pressure, sexual harassment. It says, in the same breath, that this is a signal, not a verdict. She saves it. The timeline keeps her words apart from the machine’s note. Then she chooses. The platform’s report form. StopNCII, for intimate images we never ask her to upload. Or the GBV Command Centre, 0800 428 428, social workers in every official language. Nothing leaves the phone unless she shares it. Leave quickly hides the screen. The record stays.

She does not pay. A university, an employer, or a shelter pays for a staff desk and for counts she opts into. Grants and company social investment keep the public line free. We do not sell records. We do not charge for the call.

You are not buying another hotline. You are buying the file that is still there on Monday.

## How the desk makes money

Lean canvas. Say it only if a judge asks.

- Problem: the evidence is scattered, and tens of thousands of cases are withdrawn.
- Customer: she uses it free. The buyer is a campus gender office, an employer, or a shelter.
- Offer: a staff desk, referral notes she opts into, and anonymised counts. Not her messages.
- Price: an annual desk licence. Grants and company social investment fund the public tier.
- Never: ads on a record, sale of files, a fee to reach 0800 428 428.
- Cost: hosting, review of the signal check, and partnerships with organisations that already answer the phone.
- Measure: records she completes, shares she chooses, and desks that renew.

## Demo order

1. Open the site. Read the sentence, then one South African figure, then the three steps.
2. Open the app on the demo account. Open the sample incident. The banner says it is not a real person.
3. Start a new incident. Paste: “I know your class. Post it or I send the pictures to your mother.” Check the message. Save. The timeline gains a line. Alerts gains one item.
4. Tap 0800 428 428. Share the record. Leave quickly. The cover says Notes.
5. Then read the two speeches above.

The password is not in this file.
